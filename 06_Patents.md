# Chapter 6: Patents

---

## What is a Patent?

A patent is a **statutory right granted by the government** to an inventor for an invention that satisfies patentability requirements. It gives the patentee an **exclusive right to prevent others** from making, using, selling, offering for sale, or importing the patented product/process without authorization.

---

## Patentability Requirements

For an invention to qualify for patent protection in India, it must satisfy:

1. **Novelty** – The invention must be new (not already part of prior art)
2. **Inventive Step** – Must involve a technical advance; not obvious to a person skilled in the art
3. **Industrial Applicability** – Must be capable of being made or used in an industry
4. **Must Not Fall Under Excluded Subject Matter** – Not excluded under Sections 3 or 4 of the Patents Act

### Understanding Novelty
An invention lacks novelty if already publicly disclosed via existing patents, research papers, websites, conference papers, public demonstrations, or existing products.

### Understanding Inventive Step
Not just an obvious modification. Example: Simply changing the external color of a phone is NOT an inventive step. But developing a technically improved battery architecture that solves a recognized problem MAY satisfy it.

### Industrial Applicability
The invention must have a **practical industrial use**, not just be theoretical. Examples: Manufacturing process, Medical device, Agricultural machine, Electronic circuit.

---

## What Can a Patent Protect?

- **Product Invention** – A new technical product (e.g., new type of battery)
- **Process Invention** – A new technical process (e.g., new industrial method for making a chemical)
- **Improvement** – A new technical improvement to existing technology (if it meets requirements)

---

## Non-Patentable Inventions (Sections 3 & 4)

Not every new idea qualifies. Indian law excludes:

| Section | What is Excluded |
|---|---|
| 3(c) | Mere discovery of a scientific principle or abstract theory |
| 3(d) | New forms of known substances without enhanced efficacy |
| 3(i) | Medical, surgical, or curative treatment methods |
| 3(k) | Mathematical method, business method, computer program per se, algorithms |
| 4 | Inventions relating to atomic energy |

**Also excluded:** Frivolous inventions, methods of agriculture/horticulture, plants and animals (except microorganisms), traditional knowledge-related subject matter.

## Indian Patent Law — History

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| 1856 | Early Indian patent legislation |
| 1911 | Indian Patents and Designs Act |
| 1970 | Patents Act enacted |
| 1972 | Patents Act came into force |
| 1999, 2002, 2005 | Major amendments |
| 2005 | Product patent protection restored for pharmaceuticals |

**The 1970 Act:**
- Focused on promoting **domestic industries**
- Limited product patents in pharma/food → preferred **process patents**
- Included compulsory licensing provisions

**2005 Amendment:**
- Introduced **product patent protection** in pharmaceuticals (TRIPS compliance)
- Strengthened patent examination

---

## Software and Computer Programs

- **Section 3(k):** Computer program per se, mathematical methods, business methods, and algorithms are **NOT patentable**
- BUT: A software-related invention with a **technical effect/contribution** may potentially be patentable if it satisfies all requirements

| | Copyright | Patent |
|---|---|---|
| Protects | Source code, object code, documentation | Technical invention with qualifying contribution |
| Example | Source code of an app | Technical system with software that solves a technical problem |

---

## AI-Based Inventions

**Potentially patentable:**
- AI-controlled industrial systems
- Novel hardware architecture
- Technical image-processing systems
- AI-based signal-processing techniques
- Technical improvements in computing systems

**Potentially problematic:**
- Mathematical model alone
- Algorithm alone
- Abstract AI concept
- Business method implemented using software

> **Key:** AI + Technical Contribution + Patentability Requirements = May be patentable
